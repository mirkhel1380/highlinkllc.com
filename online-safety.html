Practical guidance

A safer everyday routine

These are general security practices for personal and business accounts.

Accounts

Protect important logins

Use unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication where available.

  • Avoid reusing passwords
  • Review recovery information
  • Use additional verification
Devices

Keep software current

Updates often include security fixes, so keep operating systems, browsers, and important apps up to date.

  • Install trusted updates
  • Remove software you no longer use
  • Lock devices when unattended
Browsing

Be careful with links and downloads

Unexpected messages and fake login pages are common ways people lose account access.

  • Check the website address
  • Avoid unknown downloads
  • Be cautious with urgent messages
Business

Limit access to what people need

For business tools, use separate accounts, appropriate permissions, and clear access policies.

  • Use role-based access where possible
  • Remove access when no longer needed
  • Keep important recovery methods current

Build a practical security routine for a small team

Online safety starts with repeated habits. For a small business, that often means protecting shared accounts, knowing who is allowed to access company tools, updating software, and having a recovery process if a device is lost or an account is locked.

You do not need to purchase every security product to begin. Start with the accounts and devices that would disrupt your operations most if they were unavailable.

Key things to check

Accounts and access

  • Use unique strong passwords for important services
  • Enable multifactor authentication where available
  • Remove unused team access promptly
  • Keep recovery contacts and account ownership current

Devices and recovery

  • Apply official security updates
  • Keep backups of essential business information
  • Use trusted app sources and browser downloads
  • Review suspicious login notices and access requests

If a message pressures you to act urgently, independently verify the sender through an established contact method before providing information or approving account changes.

Frequently asked question

How often should a business review basic security?

Review access when people join or leave a team and whenever important systems change. Periodic checks help catch outdated permissions and recovery details.
