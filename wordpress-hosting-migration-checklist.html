Moving WordPress hosting without losing track of important details

Hosting migrations involve more than copying a website. A business website may rely on DNS settings, email accounts, forms, online payments, analytics, caching, and backups. A careful checklist can help you understand the move even if a hosting company performs the technical migration for you.

This guide focuses on decisions to make before moving an existing WordPress site. It is not a technical migration script and does not assume that every business should switch providers.

BEFORE THE MOVE

1. Record what your site currently needs

Website inventory

  • Current WordPress version and major plugins.
  • Approximate site and database size.
  • Business forms, store checkout, or membership functions.
  • Any scheduled jobs or nonstandard server requirements.

Connected services

  • Domain registrar and DNS management location.
  • Business email provider and mail routing.
  • Analytics, Search Console, and site verification details.
  • External integrations and API-dependent features.

If your site has a store, bookings, or customer accounts, plan how new orders and updates will be handled during the transition. Migrations should be planned around actual business activity rather than only around a convenient technical window.

COMPARE PROVIDERS

2. Ask these questions before selecting a WordPress host

  1. Who manages the migration? Confirm whether the host performs the transfer or requires you to arrange it.
  2. What counts as an eligible migration? Ask about domain changes, multiple websites, custom configurations, and possible extra charges.
  3. What does the plan include? Review website limits, resources, backup retention, security options, and support.
  4. What happens at renewal? Compare the full ongoing price, not just introductory promotions.
  5. How is email handled? Confirm whether mailboxes, DNS records, and third-party email services are covered.
  6. What is the fallback? Understand how to restore the old working version if something does not go as planned.

There is no universal best host. Managed WordPress plans can suit owners who value hands-on help, while general-purpose or cloud hosting may be better for specialized technical requirements.

QUALITY CHECK

4. Test the essential customer journeys

After the host or your technical team reports that the migration is ready, review the site as a customer would before considering the move finished.

  • Check the homepage and important service or product pages.
  • Test navigation, contact forms, image loading, and download links.
  • If the site accepts orders, test the supported checkout process safely in a designated test mode.
  • Confirm important redirects and canonical URLs still point to the right pages.
  • Verify SSL, email delivery and your domain/DNS settings with the responsible provider.
  • Keep the old working site available until the migration has been confirmed.

Existing Google rankings are not guaranteed to remain unchanged after a hosting move. Avoid changing site structure and URLs unnecessarily during migration, and monitor real website behavior once the transition is complete.

AFTER THE MOVE

5. Monitor the first days after migration

Watch for broken forms, unexpected error pages, missing emails, and site performance issues. Compare the actual customer experience with your pre-migration checks. Review Search Console for important crawl or indexing problems, and confirm that backups can be accessed and restored through the new provider.

Do I have to change my domain when moving hosts?

No. In many cases you can keep your existing domain and update hosting-related configuration. Confirm the exact process with your registrar and new provider.

Will the new host automatically manage business email?

Not always. Check the availability and scope of email support and verify your existing mailbox and DNS setup separately.

Is faster hosting enough to improve SEO?

Hosting can affect website experience and reliability, but helpful content, crawlability, site structure, and many other factors also matter.
