The local SEO checklist: start with accurate information

Local search is about helping nearby people identify the right business and understand where, when, and how to contact it. Before purchasing any platform, confirm that your business has an eligible local presence and that your information is correct. A listing tool can help organize updates, but it cannot replace accurate business data or thoughtful customer service.

This checklist is written for business owners, store managers, and teams that manage one or more physical locations or qualifying local service areas. An online-only brand without an eligible local business presence should usually start with general website SEO research rather than a local listings subscription.

STEP 01

Write down one official version of your business details

Core details to confirm

  • Public business name used in the real world.
  • Correct customer-facing address or eligible service area.
  • Working phone number and website address.
  • Accurate main business category and services.

Helpful extras

  • Normal hours and holiday hours.
  • Short description written for real customers.
  • Current logo and genuinely representative photos.
  • Booking or contact page, where appropriate.

Avoid: Adding keywords that are not part of your real business name, inventing locations, or using addresses where your company does not qualify to be listed. Misleading details can confuse customers and conflict with platform rules.

STEP 02

Audit the listings customers actually encounter

  1. Find existing profiles. Search for your business name and phone number. Identify the profiles customers can currently access.
  2. Verify ownership where possible. Use each platform's normal verification process rather than creating duplicate profiles.
  3. Correct the essentials. Fix inconsistent names, inaccurate hours, wrong phone numbers, and outdated website links.
  4. Review duplicate listings. Request proper corrections or merges through the relevant platform's tools if duplicate profiles are causing confusion.
  5. Keep a simple change log. Record the date and type of each important edit so your team knows which version is current.

For a business with multiple locations, work through each address separately. The opening hours, phone number, customer entrance, and services might differ from one location to another.

STEP 03

Build a genuine customer review workflow

Reviews are not just a marketing metric. Read them to learn where customers have questions or concerns. Reply respectfully and avoid sharing private customer information in public responses.

  • Encourage feedback from genuine customers without offering misleading incentives or controlling whether reviews are positive.
  • Respond to common questions consistently and accurately.
  • Use critical feedback to improve opening hours, service descriptions, or customer communication.
  • Review the policies of each platform before using any review request process.
STEP 04

Measure actions that help your business

A profile view is useful context, but it is not the same as a customer. Track a small set of practical outcomes, such as qualified calls, direction requests when relevant, booking inquiries, or visits to the correct location page. Record an initial baseline and compare changes over time.

Weekly check

  • Have hours or contact details changed?
  • Are new customer reviews waiting for a reply?
  • Are key listings showing correct information?

Monthly check

  • Which listing details need correction?
  • Are customers reaching the right website pages?
  • Are inquiries relevant to the services you offer?

When might Moz Local help?

Moz Local is designed to help eligible location-based businesses manage listing information, monitor reviews, and work with local visibility data. Its available functionality depends on plan and region. Moz's published documentation currently describes purchased local listing support for businesses in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom; verify current eligibility before paying.

Start with a manual audit if you have one small location and very few updates. Consider a dedicated platform when you need ongoing synchronization, multiple eligible locations, or a more organized review workflow. Frequently asked questions

Do I need paid local SEO software right away?

Not necessarily. Begin with accurate business details and the profiles your customers actually use. A paid platform can make ongoing management easier for some businesses.

Is Moz Local the same as Moz Pro?

No. Moz Local concentrates on local listings and related local visibility workflows. Moz Pro focuses on broader website SEO research, analysis, and tracking.

Can a listing tool promise top map rankings?

No. Consistent details can support a stronger local presence, but no software can guarantee particular search placement.
