How to Register an LLC in the USA: A Beginner's Guide

Starting a Limited Liability Company can be an important step for entrepreneurs who want to build a formal business presence in the United States. This guide explains the main steps involved in forming an LLC and what to consider before getting started.

Important: LLC requirements, fees, taxes, and reporting rules can vary by state. This article provides general educational information and is not legal or tax advice.

What Is an LLC?

An LLC, or Limited Liability Company, is a common business structure in the United States. It can provide a legal separation between the business and its owners while offering flexibility in how the company is managed.

LLCs are used by many small businesses, online businesses, consultants, e-commerce companies, and international entrepreneurs.

How to Register an LLC in the USA

STEP 1

Choose the State for Your LLC

LLCs are registered at the state level, so the first decision is choosing where your business will be formed.

Business owners should consider registration costs, annual requirements, taxes, where the business operates, and whether registering in another state would create additional obligations.

STEP 2

Choose a Business Name

Your LLC needs a name that meets the rules of the state where you register it. The name normally needs to be distinguishable from other businesses already registered in that state.

It is also useful to check whether a matching website domain and social media name are available before making your final decision.

STEP 3

Choose a Registered Agent

Most LLCs need a registered agent in the state of formation. The registered agent receives official government and legal correspondence on behalf of the company.

Some business owners act as their own registered agent when eligible, while others use a professional registered agent service.

STEP 4

File Your LLC Formation Documents

The main formation document is usually filed with the state agency responsible for business registrations. The exact document name can vary by state.

You may need to provide information such as the LLC name, registered agent, business address, management structure, and organizer information.

STEP 5

Create an Operating Agreement

An operating agreement explains how the LLC will be managed and can describe ownership, responsibilities, decision-making, distributions, and other internal rules.

Requirements differ by state, but having a clear operating agreement can be useful even for some single-member LLCs.

STEP 6

Apply for an EIN

An Employer Identification Number, commonly called an EIN, is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS.

Businesses may need an EIN for tax administration, hiring employees, opening certain business accounts, and other business activities.

STEP 7

Open a Business Bank Account

Keeping business finances separate from personal finances can make bookkeeping, accounting, and business management easier.

Banks and financial providers have their own eligibility and verification requirements, so the documents required can vary.

STEP 8

Maintain Your LLC

Forming the company is only the beginning. Depending on the state and business, ongoing obligations may include reports, taxes, licenses, registered agent service, record keeping, or renewal requirements.

Can a Non-US Resident Form an LLC?

A person does not necessarily need to live in the United States to explore forming a US LLC. International founders commonly research US business formation for e-commerce, consulting, software, digital services, and other online businesses.

However, international ownership can involve additional banking, tax, reporting, identification, and compliance considerations. Non-resident founders should make sure they understand the requirements that apply to their situation.

What Documents May You Need?

The documents needed depend on the state, service provider, financial institution, and type of business. Common business documents can include:

  • LLC formation or registration documents
  • Operating agreement
  • EIN documentation
  • Identification documents
  • Registered agent information
  • Business address information
  • Business licenses when applicable

How Long Does LLC Registration Take?

Processing times vary by state and may also depend on filing method, government workload, and whether expedited processing is available.

Because processing times can change, it is better to check the current information provided by the state where you plan to register.

How Much Does It Cost to Start an LLC?

LLC costs vary by state. Business owners may encounter state filing fees, registered agent costs, licenses, annual or periodic fees, and optional professional service fees.

Before registering, compare both the initial formation cost and the ongoing cost of maintaining the company.

LLC Registration Checklist

  1. Choose the state where you want to form your LLC.
  2. Select an available business name.
  3. Choose a registered agent.
  4. File the required formation documents.
  5. Prepare an operating agreement when appropriate.
  6. Apply for an EIN if required.
  7. Set up business banking and financial records.
  8. Check licenses, taxes, and ongoing state requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a registered agent for an LLC?

Registered agent requirements depend on the state, but LLCs commonly need a registered agent with an eligible address in the state of registration.

Do I need an EIN for my LLC?

Whether an EIN is required depends on your business structure and activities. Many businesses obtain an EIN for tax and business administration purposes.

Can I register an LLC online?

Many states provide online business filing options, while business formation services can also assist with preparing and submitting registration documents.

Which state is best for an LLC?

There is no single state that is best for every business. The right choice depends on where you operate, costs, taxes, reporting requirements, and your specific business situation.
